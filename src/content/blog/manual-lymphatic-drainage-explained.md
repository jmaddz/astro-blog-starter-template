Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD) is the treatment people ask me about with the
most mixed-up expectations. Sometimes it is oversold as a detox miracle. Sometimes
it is confused with a firm "lymphatic massage" that leaves you sore. It is
neither of those things, so here is what it actually is.

## What it is

Your lymphatic system is a bit like storm-water drainage: when it's working,
you never think about it, and when a drain is blocked, everything pools. MLD
is a very gentle, slow, rhythmic technique that encourages the movement of
lymph, the fluid that drains from your tissues through the lymphatic vessels.
Done properly (I trained in the Vodder tradition and have practised it since
2007) the pressure is feather-light and the pace is unhurried. It should feel
like almost nothing. First-timers often ask whether anything is actually
happening. It is!

## Who it genuinely helps

- **Post-surgical recovery**, including swelling and fluid retention after
  procedures, working with your surgical team's instructions
- **Lymphoedema support**, including post-treatment lymphoedema management in
  oncology care
- **Chronic illness and inflammatory conditions**, where gentle support of
  drainage can ease that heavy, congested feeling
- **General oedema and fluid retention**, where medical causes have been
  ruled out or are already managed

## Why the first appointment is 90 minutes

Because at least a third of it is conversation and education: how your
lymphatic system works, what MLD can and cannot change, how often is useful,
and what you can do yourself. MLD works best as a plan, not a one-off. I'd
rather you understand the drainage system than just have me poke at it.

## What it can't do

It does not "detox" you. Your liver and kidneys detox you, tirelessly and for
free, and they need no help with that. And I do not fit medical compression
garments: if you need one, I will refer you to a practitioner who is
credentialled for garment prescription, and be glad I did.
